# Phase 12 · UI review: "See it coming"

Reviewer lens: does the weather read at a glance and at 3 m on a 2560×1440 wall, and is it beautiful in service of understanding. Sources: every shot in `shots/`, `twinStorm3d.js`, the phase 12 blocks of `tripTwin.instrument.css` and `remote/remote.instrument.css`, and one live check at `localhost:4173` (All-in-one, 1200×863) to measure the inspect card. `A-storm-3-lens` shows the handle still at NOW, so the All-in-one lens state was not captured and is reviewed only from the wall shots.

## Verdict

The data is right and the colour discipline holds: steel rain, amber attention, red lightning, and SIMULATED on every element. But the one thing the room needs, how many minutes until the core reaches the hotel, sits in the middle of a 20-word sentence at body weight on every surface, and every map label is fixed at 11–12 px on a 2560 px wall, so from the door the storm is a thin orange line and some grey blocks. Five changes would make this build read in one second: a big numeral, a heavy track that ends at the hotel, labels scaled for the wall, framing that avoids the chrome, and fewer copies of the same sentence.

## The one-second read

From the door, the room should see one amber number, `25 min`, and one heavy amber arrow running from a pale storm in the south-west into the hotel. Everything else should be things you walk closer to read. To get there, give the strip on every wall a Manrope 200 numeral at 56 px, with the sentence beside it demoted to a 30 px claim and a 20 px steel evidence line. On S4, make that numeral 120 px at the top of the SEE IT COMING band, below the strip. On the map, draw the track core-to-hotel as a 7 px amber line with chevrons and its arrowhead ending on the hotel's ground ring, labelled once `~25 min` at wall size, and drop the part past the hotel to a faint dash. When the operator frames the storm, fit it into the area the chrome leaves free, pitch the camera to −38° across the track so the steel columns rise as a wall moving left to right, and show three labels at most (core, warning, lightning), sized for the wall. Then the number tells the room how long they have, the arrow shows where it is coming from, and the columns show how hard it is raining, in that order.
