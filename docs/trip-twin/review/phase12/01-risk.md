# Phase 12 review: risk and weather science

Reviewer lens: travel-risk intelligence analyst making real-time calls for a school group. I read `twinNowcast.js`, `twinNowcastLoad.js`, the phase 12 wiring in `page.js` and `remote/remote.js`, the lightning rule in `twinWeather.js`, and the screenshots. Unit tests pass (139 of 139). I also ran the pure nowcast against live IEM frames for 01:05Z and 01:25Z on 17 Oct 2026 (Austin, Washington DC, Miami, Chicago). I decoded the IEM n0q palette to dBZ to check the colour mapping.

## Verdict

The build has the right idea. Observed radar motion, an arrival time with its source, alert areas and lightning all on one line is what an operator needs in order to see a storm coming. Four defects can mislead the operator today:

- The headline and level come from the earliest core, not the worst one.
- The arrival time counts from the radar frame, not from now.
- The colour decoding and the motion both take in non-rain echo (clear air, birds, noise).
- Core size is modelled as a circle, which breaks on squall lines.

The fix is to run the arrival on every core pixel, from the time now, and measure "time until within 6 mi of the group" rather than "time until the core is at the hotel". Lightning from the nowCOAST density grid has the wrong timestamp and does not support a 30-minute-rule call. Show its real time window and plan for strike points.

## Findings

| # | Severity | Finding | Exact fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | P0 | The headline and level come from the earliest-arriving core, whatever its size or band. Live DC, 01:25Z: the line read "Moderate rain core · 9 mi SW · … about 11 min", level watch, from an 8-pixel speck. Behind it, a heavy core arrives at 17 min and a 377-pixel heavy core at 22 min, so the level should have been attention. | Set the level from the most severe band among all cores arriving within 60 min. Headline that core; if a weaker core arrives first, add "first rain in about 11 min". |
| 2 | P0 | The ETA counts from the frame time. The nowcast reloads every 3 min, and IEM frames are 3–10 min old when shown, so "about 25 min" can really be 15. In Europe the H SAF frame is about 65 min old, so an ETA there describes the past. The wall's fictional clock (21:33) next to a real radar time (20:21) makes the age impossible to read. | Compute `etaNow = etaMin − (now − frameAt)` and recount it every 30 s. Put the age in minutes on every line ("radar 20:17 CDT, 6 min old"). When the frame is more than 15 min old, show no ETA: "satellite estimate 65 min old · too old to time arrival". |
| 3 | P0 | Hue decoding mislabels the ends of the IEM n0q palette. Palette indices 1–48 (−32 to −8.5 dBZ) are greyish colours whose hues map to 0.95, 0.82, 0.72 and 0.55, which means "intense", "heavy" and "moderate" cores. The WMS does return these pixels: 518 across CONUS at 01:25Z, 34 in the Chicago box. At the top, 74+ dBZ (giant-hail cyan) maps to 0.12, below light rain. The real core threshold is 37 dBZ, not 40, and "heavy" starts at 43.5 dBZ. So the claim that clear-air return never becomes a storm does not hold. | Decode by exact palette lookup (index → dBZ = index × 0.5 − 32.5) instead of by hue. Set fixed thresholds: core ≥ 40 dBZ, heavy ≥ 50, intense ≥ 60. Treat anything under 20 dBZ as not rain. |
| 4 | P0 | Motion is measured on the whole field, including blue and green echo. Live Austin, 01:25Z: 81% of the box was lit at 10–30 dBZ while KAUS reported Clear (an October dusk bird bloom). The nowcast labelled 7.5 km/h NE with score 0.90 "observed motion", and any real cell would have inherited it. That frame also came close to the "lit > 90% means no-data" rejection. | Correlate only pixels ≥ 35 dBZ, and require at least 50 such pixels in both frames and a score ≥ 0.5. Otherwise say "motion not measured (too little storm echo)". The better source is the MRMS QC'd mosaic (via nowCOAST or IEM), which removes biological echo and clutter. |
| 5 | P0 | A core's extent is an equal-area circle around its weighted centroid. A squall line 60 km × 5 km becomes a 9.8 km circle, so a site in its path 20 km along the line computes as "passing". The "12 mi SW" edge distance is also wrong for any elongated core. The simulated storm's separate blobs never exercise this case. | Advect every core pixel along the motion and take the earliest pixel within 1.5 km of the site as the ETA. Report distance as the nearest core pixel. This is about 100k points × 1 solve, which is trivial at 320². |
| 6 | P0 | "Reaches the hotel" means the core edge is within 1.5 km. Lightning and the gust front usually arrive 10–20 min before the core, so the number the operator acts on comes too late. | Make the headline the time until the nearest core pixel is within 10 km (6 mi): "within 6 mi of the hotel in 10–20 min". Keep "at the hotel in 25–40 min" second. |
| 7 | P0 | The lightning line's time is the fetch time (`at: Date.now()`, `wx.imageAt = new Date()`), not the data time. nowCOAST density is a 15-min accumulation on an 8 km grid, updated about every 15 min (layer abstract, GetCapabilities 17 Oct 01:28Z: default time 01:15Z). So "nowCOAST 20:15 · closing" can describe strikes 15–30 min old. "Closing" compares two fetches, not two windows. | Request with explicit `TIME` and label the window: "Lightning · strikes 20:00–20:15 in an 8 km cell within 13 mi SW". Compute the trend only between successive distinct `TIME` values. |
| 8 | P1 | The ETA is a single number. Extrapolation error is typically ±25% in speed and ±15° in heading, before any growth or decay. The remote pill shows "~25 MIN" for any arrival under 90 min, including moderate cores at watch level. | Run the arrival at speed ×0.75/×1.25 and heading ±15°, then show the earliest and latest: "in 15–30 min". The pill shows the same range, only at attention or higher. |
| 9 | P1 | A core needs only 3 pixels (0.75 km²) in a single frame. Live Miami, 01:25Z: "Heavy rain core over the hotel now", attention, from a 3-pixel speck 0.8 km away, while KMIA reported Partly Cloudy at 01:05Z. | Require at least 16 pixels (4 km²), and require the core to overlap a core in the older frame after shifting by the motion. Otherwise draw it but do not headline it. |
| 10 | P1 | One motion vector covers 160 km. MCSs, right-moving supercells and back-building lines move differently from the field mean. In DC the whole field gave 72 km/h at 53° and cores alone gave 63 km/h at 62°, which shifts the ETA by about 15%. | Measure motion in a 60 × 60 km window around each candidate core, with the global vector as fallback. If local and global differ by more than 30° or 30%, add "motion uncertain" and widen the ETA range. |
| 11 | P1 | Stationary and training cells are reported as "nearly stationary", level info. Cells repeating over the same place for an hour are the flash-flood setup, not a non-event. | If any core pixel has been within 10 km of the site in 4 or more of the last 6 frames, or MRMS 1-h QPE at the site is ≥ 25 mm, set attention: "Heavy rain repeating over the same area for 40 min · flash flooding possible on low ground · MRMS 20:15". |
| 12 | P1 | "No rain core within 50 mi" reads as reassurance on unstable afternoons. Gulf-air storms form overhead with 10–20 min of radar lead time, often less. | When the NWS hourly thunder chance is ≥ 20% during an outdoor item, append: "NWS: 40% chance of thunderstorms 14:00–18:00 · storms can form overhead with little radar lead time". Lightning stays the trigger. |
| 13 | P1 | Every ≥ 40 dBZ blob is called a "rain core", the same name for a thunderstorm and for bright band (cool-season stratiform), anomalous propagation or wind-farm clutter. Bright band and clutter then inherit the field motion and produce false arrivals. | Call a core a "thunderstorm core" only when lightning was seen within 10 km of its pixels in the last 15 min. Otherwise call it a "heavy rain area" and cap it at watch. |
| 14 | P1 | Radar gaps and outages show as no rain. If the nearest WSR-88D is down, or the site sits more than 150 km from a radar (shallow storms under the beam), the line still reads "No rain core within 50 mi". The `n0q_0.json` `radar_quorum` value is already fetched and ignored. | Keep a list of the WSR-88Ds covering the box. When one is not reporting, or the site is more than 150 km from the nearest, say "nearest radar KEWX not reporting · cores near the hotel may not show" and make no "no core" statement. |
| 15 | P1 | Every phase 12 distance is from the hotel. The decision is about where the children are, such as an outdoor item 0.8 mi W at the Long Center. The older EX-WX-01 rule already uses gathering points; phase 12 does not. | Run the arrival and nearest lightning for every outdoor gathering and itinerary item in the next 2 h. Headline the group with the shortest lead time: "…within 6 mi of Long Center (31 people outdoors) in 10–20 min". |
| 16 | P1 | Lead time is never set against how long it takes to get the group inside. The path engine already has walk times. | Add "walk to shelter 12 min · Hotel lobby" to the line. Raise attention when the earliest lead time to 6 mi is less than the walk time + 15 min. |
| 17 | P1 | For an alert returned by the point query, "inside" comes from the first 8 zones only. A Severe Thunderstorm Watch lists dozens of counties, so the site's county is often not among them and "the hotel is inside the area" is dropped. Only alerts covering the point appear, so a warning 15 mi upstream, the clearest signal of what is coming, stays hidden until it covers the hotel. | Set `inside = true` for every point-query result and draw the zone containing the point first. Also query `alerts/active?area=<state>&status=actual` for warning polygons within 50 mi on the storm's track: "Severe Thunderstorm Warning 15 mi SW · the hotel is on its track". Sort the list as warnings > watches > advisories > Special Weather Statements. |
| 18 | P1 | Europe uses the H SAF satellite rain estimate: IR/MW-based, about 65 min old, with tall cores displaced 5–15 km by parallax. That supports neither a motion nor an ETA. | Show no core ETA in Europe until a radar composite is wired in. Use national open radar where it is licensed (DWD, KNMI, Météo-France), or OPERA or a commercial mosaic. Until then the line reads "Europe: satellite rain estimate, 65 min old · no arrival time · lightning (MTG-LI) is the live signal". |
| 19 | P2 | Precision exceeds the data. "12.0 mi" comes from 0.5 km pixels and an equal-area circle, and "13.2 mi" from an 8 km lightning cell. | Show whole miles or km. For grid lightning, say "within 13 mi". |
| 20 | P2 | The projection runs to 60 min at full opacity. Single cells live 30–60 min, and extrapolation skill falls sharply after about 30 min. | Fade projected columns linearly from +30 to +60 min, and label the span after +30 "on the current track, low confidence". |

## Decision levels

Levels key off lead time to 6 mi (10 km) of the group and off lightning, not off rain heaviness. Red is allowed here because lightning is a hazard.

| Level | Trigger (any one) | What the operator does |
|---|---|---|
| Info (no strip) | Cores passing, receding, or more than 90 min from 6 mi. No lightning within 25 mi. Thunder chance under 20%. | Nothing new. Check again at the next outdoor item. |
| Watch (steel strip) | A core ≥ 40 dBZ reaches 6 mi of a group within 90 min (earliest bound). Lightning within 25 mi. Severe Thunderstorm or Tornado Watch, or Special Weather Statement, for the point. Thunder chance ≥ 30% during an outdoor item. | Name the shelter and walk time for each outdoor group and tell group leaders. Start no new outdoor item farther from shelter than the lead time allows. |
| Attention (amber) | Earliest lead time to 6 mi is less than walk-to-shelter + 15 min. Lightning within 10 mi and closing over two windows. A warning polygon upstream with the group on its track. Repeating heavy rain (finding 11). | Start moving outdoor groups toward shelter now. Hold coaches near the venue. Leaders confirm headcount on arrival. Keep away from creeks and low crossings. |
| Act (red, lightning) | Lightning within 6 mi (10 km) of any group, or thunder heard. A Severe Thunderstorm, Tornado or Flash Flood Warning with the group inside. | Everyone into a substantial building or hard-topped vehicle; open shelters, tents and stages do not count. Start a 30-min clock from the latest strike within 6 mi. Resume only after 30 min with no strike within 6 mi and no warning for the point. For a flash flood warning, move to higher ground and walk or drive through no water. |

## Missing, ranked

Ranked by how often each item changes a school-group decision.

1. **Lightning strike points with age, per group, with the 30-min clock.** This changes the most decisions on US spring and summer trips. Use ground-network points (Vaisala/Xweather, AEM/Earth Networks, DTN) for sub-km, flash-level timing. Free fallbacks: GOES GLM (about 8 km, about 20 s latency) in the Americas and MTG-LI flashes in Europe and Africa. nowCOAST describes its density product as awareness of thunderstorm activity, not a safety trigger.
2. **Heat stress at the group's location.** Use WBGT (NWS CONUS WBGT forecast) or heat index. At WBGT ≥ 82 °F (28 °C), add shade and water breaks. At ≥ 87 °F (30.5 °C), limit exertion. At ≥ 90 °F (32 °C), stop strenuous outdoor items. In Europe, use MeteoAlarm high-temperature levels plus apparent temperature.
3. **Wind gusts for outdoor stages, tents and boats.** Gust fronts lead storm cores by 5–15 km. Use the nearest METAR gust, the NWS hourly gust forecast, and the gust value in warning text. Typical stage plans secure at 30 mph and clear the stage at 40 mph gusts.
4. **Flash flooding along today's walking and coach paths.** It is less frequent than items 1–3 but has the highest consequence for groups in the Texas Hill Country and similar terrain. Sources: MRMS 1-h and 3-h QPE against Flash Flood Guidance, Flash Flood Warnings, and local low-water crossing closures (ATXfloods in Austin).
5. **Air quality.** Use AirNow AQI in the US and CAMS in Europe; wildfire smoke and students with asthma make this decision-relevant. At AQI ≥ 101, limit exertion for sensitive students. At ≥ 151, move items indoors.
6. **Coastal items: rip-current risk** (NWS surf zone forecast) on beach days, and the NHC cone for Caribbean and Gulf trips.
7. **Visibility and road state for coach movements.** Dense fog advisories, METAR visibility under 1/4 mi, and ice.
8. **Cold.** Wind chill, snow and ice for winter and ski trips.
9. **UV index.** Pack and brief only; it rarely changes a plan.
