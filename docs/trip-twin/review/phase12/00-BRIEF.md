# Phase 12 review brief: "See it coming"

**Goal (Jesse, 16 Oct 2026):** give the SafeTrekr operations team the most relevant information to make real-time weather decisions. "Being able to see a storm coming is more important than pretty graphics; done well, the graphics enhance understanding of what people on the ground face." The decisions served: hold or move an outdoor item; shelter in place or walk to the rally point; which path in rain; lightning inside the 30-minute rule.

**Built (commit f54e35b on `jesse/world-view-visual-layer`, gods-eye-view):**
1. **Storm arrival line.** From the last two observed radar frames 20 min apart (IEM NEXRAD n0q WMS-T over a 160 km box at 500 m/px in the US; EUMETSAT H SAF satellite rain estimate in Europe, about 65 min behind). Motion by normalised cross-correlation (coarse-to-fine). Cores are connected areas at or above yellow (~40 dBZ); green (20–35 dBZ) is light rain, never a core, so night-time clear-air return is never a storm. Approach geometry: the earliest core whose edge comes within 1.5 km of the site on its current track, within 120 min; otherwise the nearest core (passing, moving away, stationary). Text example: "Heavy rain core · 12 mi SW · moving NE 25 mph · on this track reaches the hotel in about 25 min · radar 20:17 CDT, observed motion". Level: attention (amber) when a heavy or intense core arrives within 60 min; watch otherwise.
2. **Radar with height.** Moderate and above as translucent columns in a steel ramp (never path blue); height encodes the intensity class, not cloud height, and every inspect card says so; within 3 km of the site columns become faint flat plates so the hotel stays readable.
3. **Time lens.** One scrub (All-in-one timeline; on the walls via the remote's buttons −30, Now, +15…+60, session field `wxAt`): observed frames back two hours, then a labelled projection on the current track up to 60 min ahead ("not a forecast model"), then the model forecast map and the site forecast. Every wall shows "VIEWING 20:48 CDT · …" while the lens is away. People and incidents stay live (dimmed).
4. **Official alert areas.** NWS alerts for the point, with the alert polygon or its forecast zones (up to 8), drawn as low amber walls; lines say "the hotel is inside the area". Europe (MeteoAlarm) has no shapes; said so.
5. **Lightning with distance and trend.** The nearest lit cell of the density grid (nowCOAST 8 km cells / EUMETSAT MTG-LI 2 km): "Lightning · 8 km grid cell 13.0 mi SW · nowCOAST 20:15 · closing".
6. **Inspect card.** Click any column, the track, an alert area or the lightning line: value, source, age, place.
7. **Simulated storm (demo).** Prototype › Simulated storm approaching, or the remote's 03 Weather › Demo (session field `simStorm`, a start time so every wall draws the same storm): a squall line moving NE at 40 km/h, its strongest core reaching the site about 30 min after the start, simulated strikes riding with it, and a SIMULATED Severe Thunderstorm Warning box. Every element says SIMULATED.

**Where it shows:**
- **All-in-one:** a rail line (amber or steel), an approach chip that frames the storm and turns radar 3D on, the track and columns on the map, timeline with a "projection on the current track" segment, inspect card.
- **Every wall:** a strip under the header while a core is arriving or over the site, or while the lens is away.
- **S4 Weather & alerts:** "SEE IT COMING · OBSERVED MOTION" band first, then OFFICIAL (with the inside flag), SENSED (heat, nearest lightning), NOW. Radar 3D on by default.
- **Remote 03 Weather (now live):** approach line, time lens for every wall, official alerts, lightning, demo storm, "Frame the storm on S4". The Weather tile carries an amber "~25 MIN" pill. The remote computes the nowcast itself from the same sources (`twinNowcastLoad.js`, no Cesium).

**Files:** `src/operations/tripTwin/twinNowcast.js` (pure, tested in `twinNowcast.test.mjs`), `twinNowcastLoad.js` (fetching), `twinStorm3d.js` (Cesium layer), wiring in `page.js` (search "phase 12"), `twinScreen.js` (wall strip), `twinHud.js` (rail line), `remote/remote.js` (refreshWx) and `remote/stations.js` (function weather), styles in `tripTwin.instrument.css` and `remote/remote.instrument.css`.

**Hard rules:** green only approved places; blue only paths; red only incidents and hazards (lightning, fire); amber attention and official alerts. Never "safe", "all clear", "missing", "escape route", "exit" (for entrances), "corridor", "will" for forecasts; "path" not "route". Every number carries its source and age. Data snaps, presentation moves; the operator owns the camera (framing the storm is an operator action). Walls show no controls.

**Known limits (deliberate this phase):** one motion vector for the whole field (no per-cell tracking); the projection is an extrapolation and stops at 60 min; lightning is a density grid, not strike points; Europe's rain estimate is about 65 min behind real time; the sample clock on the walls is fictional while weather times are real (both are labelled).
